## Notes

- **D1:** most RGB listings sell common anode and cathode, diffused and clear, in one listing.
  Pick **common anode** and **diffused** explicitly. Skip the square "2×5×5 mm", straw-hat and
  piranha types. A diffused LED gives an even glow through the panel. Check the anode lead
  (longest) against the footprint when fitting.
- **J1, J2:** some listings also offer right-angle ("curved needle") headers. Pick straight.
  Several popular box header listings skip 8P entirely.
- **Cable type:** the TLZWLA listing has crimp types A–F. Choose by the pin-mapping picture, not
  the seller's "same/opposite direction" label. A, C and F are wired 1→1, and B, D and E are
  reversed. **Type F** has both sockets the same way round with a flat ribbon. It matches the
  board, where J1 and J2 have the same rotation.
- **Cable length:** CHAIN_OUT (J2) and CHAIN_IN (J1) sit side by side in the board's top-left
  corner, OUT left of IN, 12 mm apart. At 10 cm pitch with module 2 left of module 1 (top
  view), OUT to the next module's IN is 88 mm, centre to centre. A 10 cm cable (about 94 mm
  centre to centre, if the 10 cm is measured end to end) runs nearly straight along the top
  edge with a few mm of slack. Take 10 cm, the shortest length the listings offer; it is
  the right length, not just enough. A shorter cable won't reach.
- **Resistors:** single-value listings like this one usually charge the same for every value,
  but only the 51R price was seen.
- **Mounting stack:** from design.md, *Enclosure*. Each board corner is a column from plate
  to plate: an M3 × 8 screw through the 3 mm front plate into a 25 mm female-female standoff,
  a 5+6 mm male-female standoff under the PCB with its stud up through the board into the
  long one, and an M3 × 6 screw up through the back plate into the short standoff. There are no case spacers. The footswitch nuts and washers come with the
  switch. Use brass or steel standoffs, not nylon: they carry the stomping load. One 100-pack
  of each screw length covers 8 modules (32 each).
- **Sockets:** round-hole (machined) sockets hold the chips more firmly, which helps in a box
  that gets stomped on. Either type works.
